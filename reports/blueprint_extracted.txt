Research Execution Blueprint for a Background-
Adjusted, Cross-City ATM-Site Crime Study
Executive summary
The attached material supports a clear redesign: stop extending the earlier SEM–decision-tree–SHAP–
FIS–Choquet pipeline and instead build a rigorously defined, exposure-aware ATM-site prediction
study in New York City, Buffalo, and Rochester. The central question should be whether the immediate
environment of an ATM site contains predictive information beyond ordinary surrounding crime, and
whether that incremental signal survives transfer to another city. The longer 29-page document contains
the full rationale and design; the shorter attachment substantially restates its later execution and fallback
plan. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file0


The strongest contribution is therefore not a novel classifier. It is a clean empirical test of three things:
incremental ATM-site risk, external transportability, and the value of limited target-city data. This
direction is well aligned with prior cross-city crime research: Bappee et al. explicitly treated Toronto and
Vancouver as source domains and Halifax as a target domain, found source-only transfer weaker than
configurations incorporating target examples, and documented the possibility of negative transfer when
domains were dissimilar. 1 More sophisticated unsupervised cross-city adaptation has also been
demonstrated, so there is no need to make deep domain adaptation the first version of this paper. 2
Cross-jurisdictional place-based research likewise finds that environmental risk factors may recur across
cities without having identical effects, which is precisely the scientific pattern this ATM study should test.
 3




The largest unresolved issue is ATM exposure history. The New York State Department of Financial
Services dataset is an excellent common denominator because it is statewide, has individual ATM locations,
is posted monthly, and specifically covers bank-owned or in-bank ATMs. However, its public metadata
describes the time period as current, not as a historical panel, and explicitly excludes independent non-
bank ATMs such as machines in delis and bodegas. As of September 15, 2026, that dataset had been
updated during the month immediately preceding this report. 4 A retrospective ATM-year study must
therefore first determine whether past monthly snapshots can be recovered. A current September 2026
ATM list must not simply be assumed to represent the ATM universe in 2022–2025.


The second critical issue is outcome harmonization. The paper should study ATM-site acquisitive crime
risk, not claim to identify literal attacks on ATM machines unless police records support that interpretation.
NYPD records are complaints, with detailed offense descriptors and location fields, and their own
documentation notes that complaint classifications reflect the most serious offense and that geocoding/
report timing have limitations. 5 Buffalo exposes incident date/type/description fields, while Rochester
publicly exposes Part I categories such as robbery, burglary, and larceny. 6 Consequently, a common
codebook must be created before model fitting, with fraud, forgery, account compromise, and other
nonphysical mechanisms separated from the primary endpoint.

A realistic baseline execution period is approximately 14–16 weeks if historical ATM exposure can be
resolved quickly. If it cannot, there are only three scientifically honest options: obtain historical records from
DFS/platform archives; switch to a prospective outcome window beginning from a known ATM snapshot; or
reduce the paper to a more limited design while explicitly weakening exposure claims. The worst option is
silently joining a 2026 ATM list to many prior years of crime.


Research architecture and decision branches
The confirmatory study should formalize the attached plan as a comparison between background crime
and incremental site context. fileciteturn0file1 Define, for ATM site i and prediction period t:



                Yit = {
                          count of qualifying incidents within r of site i,      count design
                          1{at least one qualifying incident occurs},            binary design

with a preregistered primary radius r = 50 m and sensitivity radii of 25 m and 100 m.


Define lagged background crime as:




                                                         2
   Bi,t−1 = qualifying incidents during the previous period in an outer annulus, e.g. 200–500 m,

so that the outcome buffer itself is not recycled into the baseline predictor. This is the central design
innovation in the attachments. fileciteturn0file1


The confirmatory model ladder should be:


                                               M0 : Yit ∼ Bi,t−1

                                            M1 : Yit ∼ Bi,t−1 + Xi

where M1 is regularized logistic regression for binary outcomes or Negative Binomial regression for counts,
and


                                           M2 = fboost (Bi,t−1 , Xi )

using either XGBoost or LightGBM. Both are established gradient-boosting systems; their use should be
described as a flexible tabular benchmark rather than a methodological novelty. 7


For transfer, define:


                                   Transfer Gap = Plocal-full − Pzero-shot ,

and


                          Transfer Benefit(k) = Psource+k% target − Pk% target only .

The second quantity is essential. Improvement after adding 10% target data is not evidence that transfer
helped unless source + 10% target also beats a model trained on the same 10% target data alone. This
comparison mirrors an important lesson from Bappee et al., where adding suitable target data materially
changed cross-domain performance and poorly related sources could produce negative transfer. 8

The staffing structure is unspecified in the attachments. The instructions below therefore use functional
roles: PI/lead researcher, data/GIS analyst, statistical/ML analyst, crime-domain reviewer, and QA/
reproducibility reviewer. One person can hold several roles in a low-resource project, but the primary crime
codebook and a sample of event–ATM matches should ideally receive an independent second review.

Recommended target-codebook rule. The main outcome should include only events that can defensibly
be interpreted as physical/acquisitive incidents in the ATM micro-environment. Purely transactional fraud,
bank-account compromise, forgery, identity theft, and electronically mediated loss should not be mixed into
the physical-site endpoint merely to enlarge the sample. The attached plan reaches the same conclusion
after noting the heterogeneity of the existing NYC ATM-premise complaints. fileciteturn0file1


The event audit should be expanded beyond the original suggestion of approximately 100 NYC matches. A
stronger implementation is a stratified review across all included cities, oversampling ambiguous
offense categories and cases near 25/50/100 m thresholds. Record event ID, site ID, distance, native
description, common category, fraud/transaction flag, duplicate flag, location plausibility, and reviewer
decision. A second reviewer should independently code a subset and disagreements should be adjudicated
before the final codebook is locked.

Validation hierarchy. The principal result should come from a deployment-realistic split, not random cross-
validation. Use training on earlier periods and testing on later periods; add spatial holdouts such as
neighborhood/grid/precinct groups; then rotate a completely held-out city. Spatially structured random
splits can give optimistic error estimates because neighboring observations are dependent. 16


All learned preprocessing belongs inside the training folds: missing-value imputation, scaling, feature
selection, hyperparameter optimization, class weighting/resampling, and probability calibration. A final test
city/time block must never influence these operations.


For binary outcomes, designate AUPRC as the primary discrimination metric if the positive class is rare;
precision-recall analysis is more informative than relying only on ROC curves under strong class imbalance.
 26 Also report ROC-AUC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope or calibration plot, top-5% and top-10%

recall, precision in the top risk fraction, and lift. For counts, report Negative Binomial/Poisson deviance as
appropriate, MAE, calibration of predicted counts, and site-ranking performance.


Do not bootstrap individual ATM-period rows naively when the same ATM appears repeatedly. For
uncertainty, resample at least at the ATM/site level; where residual spatial dependence remains material,
use a spatial-block bootstrap. Model-to-model comparisons should be paired so both models are evaluated
on identical held-out observations.


For logistic regression, standardization parameters must be learned from training data only. For zero-shot
transfer, the safest primary representation uses physically interpretable common units. A sensitivity analysis
may use city-standardized variables, but because target-city feature distributions are then used in
constructing the representation, that analysis should be labeled explicitly as a transductive/domain-
normalized variant.
